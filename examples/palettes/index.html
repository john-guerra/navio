Categorical colour when there are more categories than colours

Navio colours a categorical column with d3.scaleOrdinal(d3.schemeCategory10). That scheme holds ten colours and scaleOrdinal recycles them, so the eleventh category is drawn in exactly the colour of the first — silently. This page draws nutrients.csv, whose group column has 25 categories, once per candidate palette.

Click any column header to sort by it. Sort by a palette column and every group becomes one contiguous band. Sort by calories or water and the groups shatter into single-pixel rows in all of them at once. That is the point: for a widget drawing one pixel row per record, run length decides legibility more than the palette does. See docs/ai/COLOR-CATEGORICAL.md for the evidence.

How far apart the colours actually are

Minimum pairwise distance — the closest pair anywhere in the palette, which is the pair a reader confuses. Under 2.3 is below the just-noticeable difference. Two metrics, because they disagree: ΔE00 is the modern standard, ΔE*ab is what the visualisation size literature reports, and ΔE00 down-weights differences between saturated colours.

Today’s palette already collides at ten categories. schemeCategory10 scores 16.2 ΔE00 for normal vision but 1.6 under protanopia — below the just-noticeable difference, with no recycling involved. Switch “Simulate vision” to Protanopia and look at its column in the widget above.

Palettes and scores are generated by node examples/palettes/build-palettes.mjs, which validates its CIEDE2000 implementation against the Sharma, Wu & Dalal (2005) reference pairs before computing anything. Colour-vision simulation is Viénot, Brettel & Mollon (1999) on linear-light sRGB.
